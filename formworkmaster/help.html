Formwork Master calculates quantities automatically from your model. Always review the results before you use them. See the Disclaimer.

Installation

  1. Install Formwork Master from the Autodesk Design and Make Marketplace. The installer places the add-in in %AppData%\Autodesk\ApplicationPlugins\Formwork Master.bundle. One installation covers Revit 2025, 2026 and 2027.
  2. Start Revit and sign in with the Autodesk account that holds your subscription or trial (see License and working offline).
  3. Open the Add-Ins tab. Formwork Master adds a panel named Formwork Master with one button, Calculate Formwork.

Hover over the button to see a short description. Pressing F1 while the tooltip is showing opens this page.

Before you start

Supported elements

Formwork Master calculates six element types:

Switch in the windowRevit category
ColumnsStructural Columns
BeamsStructural Framing
WallsWalls
FloorsFloors
FoundationsStructural Foundations
StairsStairs (including their runs and landings)

The add-in does not check materials. Every element in these six categories is calculated, whatever it is made of: cast-in-place concrete, precast concrete, steel or timber. When you select, leave out any element that does not need formwork, such as steel beams or precast units.

The add-in also does not check what an element is used for. A finish modelled as a separate wall or floor (plaster, tiles, a floor screed) is treated like any other wall or floor: the structural faces it touches are deducted, and it gets formwork itself if you select it. Calculate on a copy of the model without these finishes, or keep them in a linked model (elements in linked models are not used for deductions).

Join elements first

Formwork Master was tested on models whose structural elements are joined. Join overlapping elements first (Revit's Join Geometry, or our free Auto Join Pro add-in); this reduces the chance of an overlap being counted twice.

Wall corners: use butt joins, not mitre joins

Where walls meet at a corner, join them with a butt (square) join rather than a mitre. A mitred corner turns the contact between walls, beams and slabs into slanted or triangular pieces. The areas are still measured from the actual geometry, but formulas gain triangle terms or become a single number, which makes them harder to check by hand.

Model the soil if faces against earth should be deducted

Faces in contact with a toposolid or topography (for example the bottom of a foundation, a ground beam or a ground-floor wall) are deducted automatically. If the soil is not modelled, those faces are included, and you decide whether to remove them (see What you decide).

Linked models, design options and phases

Only elements in the current model are used for deductions; elements in linked models are not. The add-in does not filter by phase or design option, and models that use them have not been tested. If the results look wrong in such a model, please email us with a sample file (see Support).

Ramps

Elements in the Ramps category are not calculated. Model a structural ramp as a sloped floor; sloped floors are fully supported.

Save your model

If some faces cannot be calculated, the add-in writes a small report next to your model file. If the model has never been saved, the report goes to your Windows temporary folder instead, which is harder to find.

Choosing a category

Pick a category your project does not use for anything else you schedule, and keep using the same one for the same model. Each category gets its own schedule. If you later calculate in a different category, the formwork from earlier runs stays in the old one; the add-in warns you about this (see Running it again).

Calculating

  1. Choose a category and switch on the element types you want.
  2. Click Select. The window hides, and Revit asks you to pick elements. Only the element types you switched on can be selected; you can drag a window across the whole model.
  3. Click Finish on the Options Bar. To give up instead, press Esc or click Cancel on the Options Bar: you go back to the window, the status line shows Selection cancelled., and nothing is calculated.
  4. The window comes back and the progress bar fills as each element is calculated. To stop, click Cancel or ×: the calculation stops after the current element and the window closes. Formwork already created in this run is kept.
  5. When it finishes, any warnings are shown first, then the schedule opens.

What you get

The add-in never modifies your structural elements. Columns, beams, walls, floors, foundations, stairs, their joins and their dimensions are left untouched. It only adds the following to your model.

Formwork elements

Each face that needs formwork becomes a thin solid (a Revit DirectShape) in the category you chose, with the type name Formwork Master. You can see them in the model, select them, hide them or isolate them like any other element.

Materials

Formwork is coloured by element type, using materials the add-in adds to the project: FM_Column, FM_Beam, FM_Wall, FM_Floor, FM_Foundation and FM_Stair (plus a grey FM_Formwork as a fallback). You can change their colours in the Material Browser; the add-in reuses a material that already exists by name, so your changes are kept on later runs.

Parameters

The add-in adds its own parameters to the three formwork categories and fills them in on every formwork element:

FM_element_typeElementColumn, Beam, Wall, Floor, Foundation or Stair.
FM_positionGridThe grid location of the structural element. Unknown if it cannot be placed on the grids.
FM_orientationOrientationTop, Side or Bottom; stairs also use Riser and Soffit (see Reading the schedule).
FM_formulaFormulaA readable formula for the face, so you can check the number by hand.

There is also a hidden parameter, FM_formula_detail, for internal use. You can ignore it.

The schedule

After the first calculation in a category, the add-in creates a schedule named Formwork Master - category name (the category name follows your Revit language) and opens it. The schedule:

  • lists only formwork elements of the Formwork Master type, so other elements in the same category stay out of it;
  • is grouped by Level, then Element, then Grid, with subtotals for each group, a count for each level and element type, and a grand total with the total count at the end.

On later runs the existing schedule is reused as it is. You can rename its columns, change its formatting or add fields, and your changes are kept. If you delete it, the next run creates a new one.

Reading the schedule

How faces are labelled (Top / Side / Bottom)

Columns, beams and floors use one rule, based on the angle between the outward direction of the face and straight up:

Angle from straight upLabelWhat it means on site
0° – 36.87°not countedConcrete stays on a gentle top surface without a form
36.87° – 66.42°TopA steep top surface that needs a top form held down
66.42° – 113.58°SideSide form
113.58° – 180°BottomA form that needs shoring underneath
  • The end faces of a column (where it sits on a footing or meets a beam or slab) are never counted, whatever their direction.
  • The end faces of a beam are always labelled Side.
  • Walls, foundations and stairs have their own rules. Stairs use Riser and Soffit; a stair soffit starts at the same 113.58° line.
  • The label never changes the area. Use it to sort or filter the schedule.

Bottom faces of walls

Every downward face of a wall is labelled Bottom and calculated. Where a floor, beam, foundation, another wall or the soil is underneath, that part is deducted. What remains is the part with nothing underneath, for example a wall that overhangs the beam below it. If a wall should sit on a slab but a Bottom row still appears, check the model for a gap between them.

How a formula is written

Every deduction is written in brackets after a minus sign. When several identical pieces are deducted, the count comes first:

  • 260 * 180 - (80 * 120): a 260 × 180 face with one 80 × 120 opening.
  • 600 * 300 - 2 * (170 * 200): two identical 170 × 200 pieces deducted.
  • A whole number written next to brackets, such as the 16 in 16 * (17.82 * 100), is a count of identical pieces, not a length.
  • On walls, a remaining correction may appear as a single number such as - (2292.4): an area (cm², or in² in imperial projects) that balances the parts that cannot be written as rectangles, typically on walls drawn with an irregular outline.

Brackets do not tell you whether a piece was cut away by another element or is part of the face's own shape; both are written the same way. Where a face cannot be described with rectangles, the formula is a single number instead (see Known limits).

Units

There is no unit setting in the add-in. It follows your project units (Manage › Project Units), and only the length unit decides how formulas are written:

Project length unitFormula
Any metric unit: millimetres, centimetres, metres, …centimetres, 2 decimal places
Feet, feet and fractional inches, inches, fractional inches, or US survey feetdecimal inches, 3 decimal places
  • The rounding and decimal places you set for length do not change the formula; the add-in uses its own fixed precision.
  • A formula gives an area in the square of its length unit: divide by 10,000 to turn cm² into m², or by 144 to turn in² into ft².
  • The Area column always follows your project's area unit, on its own.
  • The Area column uses the rounding set in your project (Manage › Project Units › Area). The default Revit structural template rounds area to whole square metres, so small faces may show as 0 m². Set the rounding to two or more decimal places. You do not need to run the add-in again.
  • The Formula column heading has no unit, so the same schedule reads correctly in either kind of project.
  • In an imperial project the formula is converted from the add-in's centimetre calculation, so evaluating it yourself can differ from the Area column by a few hundredths of a square foot on very large faces. The Area column is the measured value.

If your length and area units are set to different systems, the Formula and Area columns will be in different units:

Formula Check

Formula Check confirms that each formula adds up to the measured area. It shows one of three things:

ShowsMeaning
-The formula matches the measured area within 0.01 m² (about 0.108 ft²).
N/AThere is no formula to compare, so no check was made. This happens on curved stair faces and on curved hole walls in sloped floors (see Known limits).
a numberThe check failed; the number is the difference, formula minus measured area. A positive number means the formula gives more than the measured area.
  • The check is always made inside the add-in in metric (the centimetre formula against the measured area in m²), with the same tolerance in every project. Only the way a difference is displayed changes: in m² when the length unit is metric, in ft² when it is imperial, whatever the area unit. The number has 4 decimal places and no unit symbol.
  • Formula Check is a text field, because it holds - and N/A as well as numbers. It cannot be totalled, and sorting it sorts the text, not the values. To find faces that need a look, filter the schedule on Formula Check does not equal -.

The Area column is the quantity

Use the Area column for quantities. The formula is there to help you read and check each face: Formula Check confirms that the formula adds up to the measured area, but it cannot confirm that the formula describes the actual shape of the face. Where the add-in cannot write a formula it can trust, it shows a single number instead; check those faces against the model or the drawings if you need to.

Totals

Revit rounds each subtotal on its own, so the sum of the subtotals can differ from the grand total by one or two digits in the last decimal place. This is normal rounding, not a calculation error. Use the grand total row.

What you decide

The add-in is responsible for

  • Reading the real geometry of each element and computing the area of every formwork face.
  • Applying the deduction relationships between elements (columns, beams, walls, floors, foundations, stairs and topography) in a fixed order.
  • Producing a readable formula for each face so that you can check the number by hand.
  • Writing the results to parameters you can schedule.

You are responsible for

  • Deciding which faces belong to the scope of the work. Construction sequence, contract scope and local pricing conventions are engineering judgements, not geometry.
  • Reviewing the schedule before it goes to a client.

Faces you may need to exclude

The add-in computes every face that is not covered by another element. Whether a face actually needs formwork is a site decision. Check these before you deliver a quantity:

  • Faces in contact with earth: the bottom of ground beams, foundations and ground-floor walls. These are deducted automatically if the soil is modelled (toposolid or topography). If it is not modelled, they are included and you have to remove them.
  • Lean concrete or blinding layers: usually not part of the structural formwork.
  • Elements cast later: water stops, kerbs and similar. This depends on your sequence.
  • Wall top faces: not needed when another structure sits on top.
  • In-place or non-standard families: check whether they belong in the calculation at all.

Running it again

After you change the model

When you calculate an element again, its old formwork in the selected category is deleted and built again. Elements you do not select keep their formwork as it was.

  • Deductions depend on the elements around a face. After changing an element, calculate it and the elements around it again. The simplest way is to calculate everything again.
  • If you delete a structural element, its formwork is not removed by the add-in. Delete it yourself; the Source ID column tells you which structural element each face belonged to.

Calculating in a different category

Only the category you select is recalculated. If formwork from earlier runs exists in another category, the add-in leaves it as it was and shows a Formwork in other categories message listing it. Check that the same elements are not counted twice, and that no schedule is missing elements that were calculated in another category. To keep only one set, delete the formwork in the other categories yourself. Formwork Master never deletes anything outside the category you selected.

Changing units

  • Area unit only: you do not need to run the add-in again. Change the unit in Manage › Project Units, and the Area column changes straight away.
  • Length unit: formulas and Formula Check differences are written as text when the add-in runs, so they do not change by themselves. Run the add-in again on all elements. Formwork that is not recalculated keeps its formula in the old unit, and the schedule ends up with both.

Undo

A run can be undone with Revit's Undo, in up to three steps: the first removes the schedule, the second the formwork and its materials, and the third the parameters added to the project. The parameters do no harm if you leave them.

If some faces are skipped

After a calculation, the add-in tells you about anything that is missing from the schedule. The formwork that was created is always correct and usable.

Skipped faces

A face that should have formwork, but could not be turned into a formwork solid, is skipped and is not in the schedule. The add-in shows a Skipped faces message listing the element IDs, and writes a report named FormworkSkipped_date_time.txt.

A face is skipped when Revit itself refuses to build it. Revit has a minimum curve length of about 1/32" (0.78 mm); a face whose boundary contains a shorter edge cannot be turned into a solid, by this add-in or any other tool. The add-in reports the face instead of guessing its area.

Short edges like this usually come from the model, not from the calculation:

  • two joined elements that overlap by a fraction of a millimetre, for example after snapping to a slightly different point;
  • dimensions with small rounding differences, often from imported CAD or a change of units;
  • elements moved or edited by hand after they were joined.

A whole face can be lost this way, not only a thin strip. To fix it:

  1. Select the element (Manage › Inquiry › Select by ID).
  2. Check how it is joined to, or cut by, the elements around it.
  3. Unjoin them, or remove the tiny overlap, then run the calculation again.

The report lists the area and the shortest edge of every skipped face, and the shortest edge Revit allows (“Min. edge”). A face whose shortest edge is below Min. edge and whose area is small is only the thin strip left by the overlap. A large one is a whole face that was lost and needs fixing in the model. If the shortest edge is not below Min. edge, the cause is not known; please send us the report so we can check it.

Failed elements

If an unexpected error happens inside the add-in, the element is not calculated and the add-in shows a Failed elements message, with a report named FormworkFailed_date_time.txt. Run the calculation again first; the error may not repeat. If it does, please send us the report (see Support). Everything else in that run was calculated normally.

Where the reports are

Reports are written next to your model file, or to your Windows temporary folder if the model has not been saved. Click Open report folder in the message to find the file. A report lists element IDs, the add-in and Revit versions, and the model's file path. It stays on your computer and is not sent anywhere. You can delete it whenever you like. Reports always use centimetres, whatever the project units.

Known limits

Each item below says what you will see, why, and how to read it.

Joined elements that overlap by less than about 1/32" (0.78 mm)

This is a limit of Revit's geometry, not of the calculation: Revit cannot build a face with an edge shorter than its minimum curve length. The affected face is skipped and reported (see If some faces are skipped), and this can remove a whole face, such as a column side or a slab soffit. Unjoin the elements, or remove the overlap, and run again. The add-in never changes your model to work around it.

Small overlaps between elements (0.45 cm or less)

Where an element overlaps a face by a narrow strip 0.45 cm (4.5 mm, about 0.18 in) wide or less, the add-in treats it as a modelling tolerance: that strip is not deducted, and the formwork is not cut there. Overlaps like this usually come from small deviations in the model rather than from design dimensions. Any overlap wider than 0.45 cm is always deducted. The rule is the same for every element type, on flat and curved faces. No message is shown for this. The strip stays in the formwork, so the face is slightly larger than it should be (a strip 0.45 cm wide along a 450 cm edge adds about 0.02 m²).

Deductions that Revit cannot compute

To deduct a neighbouring element, the add-in asks Revit's geometry engine for the overlap between the two elements. In rare cases Revit cannot compute it and returns an error. This happens when the two shapes almost, but not exactly, line up, for example:

  • a face of one element that lies almost exactly on a face or edge of the other (a wall face flush with a slab edge, give or take a tiny fraction);
  • two curved faces that touch (a round column against a curved wall);
  • in-place or imported elements with irregular geometry.

The add-in then retries with the neighbour shifted by a tiny amount (0.01 cm and up), which solves most cases. If every retry fails, that neighbour is not deducted and the face is over-counted. Formula Check still shows OK, because no deduction was written. In 3D the formwork then runs into the neighbouring element. Redrawing the neighbour's edge so that it lines up exactly, or so that it is clearly apart, usually removes the problem.

If you see formwork running into another element and the model looks correct, please send it to us.

Opening sills

The bottom face of a window or door opening in a wall is counted, and labelled Top, only when it is at least 30 cm (about 12 in) above the wall's base level. Lower sills are not counted. The height is measured from the level, not from the floor slab, so on raised or lowered slabs the result may not match the site.

Very small pieces

Leftover pieces smaller than 1 cm² are ignored.

Details smaller than 1 cm²

If a face is a rectangle except for a detail smaller than 1 cm² (for example a 1 × 1 cm chamfer at a corner), the formula is written as the full rectangle. The Area column still uses the exact measured area, and the difference is well inside the Formula Check tolerance, so Formula Check shows -.

When the formula is a single number

The formula shows a length * width expression, with its deductions, whenever the face can be described with rectangles. It shows a single number instead when:

  • a floor or foundation face has a round or other non-rectangular opening, or a curved outer edge;
  • a face has slanted edges, for example a parallelogram or a corner cut at an angle;
  • the face has irregular geometry, such as corbels and the side faces of stairs;
  • a beam soffit above mitred wall corners becomes a trapezoid.

The number is the exact area measured from the geometry; only the expression is missing. Formula Check works as usual.

Slanted columns

The two end faces of a column are never counted. The other faces follow the angle rule in Reading the schedule, so on a slanted column the upper side is labelled Top or Side and the lower side Side or Bottom, depending on how far the column leans. This works with both Top/Base Cut Style options (Perpendicular and Horizontal) and with any Cross-Section Rotation. A rectangular side face shows a length * width formula measured along the face; a face shaped like a parallelogram shows a single number. The area is correct either way.

Slanted beams and floors

The area is always correct, and the labels follow the angle rule: the underside of a steep beam or slab is Bottom, and a steep top face is counted and labelled Top. The edges of a sloped floor are vertical in Revit, so they are always Side. Rectangular faces along the slope show a length * width formula.

Curved hole walls in sloped floors

Where a round hole or shaft passes through a sloped slab, the hole wall cannot be built as an exact solid. The add-in builds it as a thin triangulated shell instead:

  • The area is Revit's exact face area, and the formula shows that number.
  • Formula Check shows N/A, because there is no second value to compare against.
  • Elements touching that hole wall are not deducted from it.
  • On screen the shell may look slightly uneven in thickness; this does not affect the area.

Curved stair faces

Curved stair soffits and side faces are built the same way, so Formula Check shows N/A on them. Where a curved stair side face touches a wall, the contact area is not deducted, so that face may be slightly over-counted.

Openings in curved walls

Each opening edge that falls inside the arc of a curved wall loses a thin strip of about wall radius × 0.0005 × opening height (around 13–16 cm² for a typical wall). This is a deliberate trade: the small clearance keeps the calculation stable. The loss is always in the same direction and predictable.

Arcs between 89° and 90°

On curved elements whose arc spans 89–90°, the area is about 0.15% low. This comes from Revit's own surface calculation, not from the add-in; at every other angle tested the result is accurate to better than 0.01%.

Flat faces with curved edges

Areas of flat faces with curved edges, such as the bottom of a curved wall, are the values Revit itself reports. They can differ from a hand calculation by a few hundredths of a percent; a filled region drawn in Revit on the same outline shows the same value.

License and working offline

Your account

  • Sign in to Revit (top-right corner) with the same Autodesk account you used to subscribe or start the trial in the Marketplace. This is required even if your company uses network licenses.
  • If your company bought the seats, the purchaser has to assign one to your account; the Marketplace does not assign seats automatically.
  • Trials, billing and subscription changes are handled by Autodesk through your Autodesk account.

When the license is checked

  • The add-in checks your subscription with Autodesk the first time you click Calculate Formwork in a Revit session, not when Revit starts. This needs an internet connection; the check waits at most 5 seconds.
  • After a successful check, it does not check again until you restart Revit.
  • To check, the add-in sends only your Autodesk user ID and the app's ID to Autodesk, over HTTPS. See the Privacy Policy.
  • If your company uses a firewall, ask IT to allow apps.autodesk.com.

Working offline

After a successful online check, you can keep using the add-in without an internet connection for up to 7 days. The status line then shows Ready (offline – verified date). The next time you are online, the check is made again and the 7 days start over.

Revit itself can run offline for longer than 7 days. Between the 8th day offline and the end of Revit's own offline period, Revit keeps working but Formwork Master shows License check failed. Connect to the internet once and click Calculate Formwork again.

Uninstalling

Close Revit first. Then either run the installer you downloaded again and click Uninstall, or open Control Panel › Programs › Programs and Features and uninstall Formwork Master as you would any other application. This removes the add-in from Revit 2025, 2026 and 2027.

One small file is left behind: %AppData%\CYF BIM Studio\Formwork Master\license.dat. It only stores the result of the last license check for your account on this computer, so that the add-in can work offline. You can delete it, or the whole CYF BIM Studio\Formwork Master folder, by hand.

Formwork, materials, parameters and schedules already created in your models stay in those models. They are ordinary Revit elements and open normally without the add-in. Delete them yourself if you no longer need them.
